# Tennessee Protocol Coverage Audit

**Claiborne County EMS Protocol App**  
**Comparison date:** July 30, 2026  
**App catalog reviewed:** 96 Claiborne protocols  
**Primary state source:** Tennessee BLS/ALS State EMS Protocol Guidelines 2024–2025 (linked by the Tennessee EMS Board as “TN EMS Protocol Guidelines — Sept 2025”)

## Important source note

The July 2017 Tennessee PDF is a historical reference. The current Tennessee EMS Board page links a newer 218-page guideline. The state describes these protocols as guidance for preparing local treatment protocols and states that local users must review them for accuracy and appropriateness. Claiborne-approved standing orders and medical-director authorization remain controlling.

## Status definitions

- **Covered:** A dedicated Claiborne protocol exists.
- **Bundled:** The Tennessee topic is materially addressed inside a broader Claiborne protocol.
- **Add / strengthen:** A dedicated workflow or explicit section would improve coverage or field retrieval.
- **Local policy:** The state topic needs an agency decision, operational policy, or formulary approval rather than a direct copy.

## Highest-priority additions

| Tennessee topic | Current Claiborne coverage | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Family violence / suspected abuse | Added as UP-21 Family Violence / Abuse | Universal safeguarding workflow now covers intimate-partner violence, children, older/vulnerable adults, trafficking concerns, mandated reporting, documentation, scene safety, and destination coordination. Medical-director and agency approval remain required. |
| Sexual assault | Added as UP-22 Sexual Assault | Universal workflow now covers consent, trauma-informed care, evidence preservation, destination, competent-adult choice, child and vulnerable-adult reporting, qualifying-injury reporting, and Tennessee first-responder duties. Medical-director and agency approval remain required. |
| Chemical exposure / substance-specific HazMat | Added as TE-09 Chemical / HazMat Exposure | Master decontamination and responder-safety workflow now includes field quick cards for chlorine/chloramine, ammonia, organophosphate/carbamate, hydrogen sulfide, hydrofluoric acid, and unknown/corrosive exposures; it cross-links overdose/opioid, CO/cyanide, nerve-agent, and chemical-burn pathways. |
| Abdominal / pelvic trauma | Multiple Trauma is broad; pelvic trauma is not explicit | Add a focused abdominal/pelvic section including hemorrhage risk, pelvic stabilization criteria, pregnancy considerations, and trauma destination. |
| Eye trauma | No explicit protocol found | Add penetrating/blunt/chemical eye injury workflow with shield/irrigation distinctions and pressure-avoidance warnings. |
| Spinal cord injury / neurogenic shock | Selective spinal immobilization exists; no explicit cord injury or neurogenic shock content found | Add a focused spinal cord/neurogenic shock module linked to spinal protection and hypotension/shock. |
| Physical restraint | Behavioral protocols discuss agitation and safety, but no explicit physical-restraint workflow was found | Add least-restrictive restraint, positioning, monitoring, documentation, and reassessment requirements. |
| Medications at schools / non-formulary medication | No explicit operational pathway found | Add local-policy cards for assisting with emergency school medications and for patient-specific/non-formulary drugs with Medical Control. |
| Consent / POST / physician on scene | General Medical Control and refusal content exists; no dedicated quick reference | Add operational reference cards for consent, minors, POST documents, physician-on-scene authority, and protocol deviation. |
| Radio report templates | General minimum report elements exist | Add adult medical, pediatric, and trauma report templates as field tools. |
| Trauma assessment / destination | Destination principles exist, but state trauma references are not presented as a standalone field card | Add a Tennessee/Claiborne trauma destination checklist and concise transport-priority reference. |
| Pediatric age/weight/vitals/equipment | Pediatric calculator exists | Extend it with age-based normal vitals, hypotension thresholds, equipment sizes, and a visible length-based-system reminder. |

## Cardiac items to strengthen

| Tennessee topic | Current Claiborne coverage | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Non-cardiac chest pain | ACS protocol and Adult Pain Control exist | Add a short branch or separate quick card for dangerous non-ACS causes and protocol selection. |
| Premature ventricular contractions | No dedicated section found | Decide whether a standalone PVC protocol is still locally desired; otherwise add ectopy assessment to the wide-complex/rhythm reference. |
| Persistent/refractory VF | VF/pulseless VT and TEAM-Focused CPR exist | Add an explicit refractory-VF branch, including the agency decision on vector change and optional double-sequential defibrillation. |
| Irregular narrow-complex tachycardia / AF with RVR | Adult Narrow-Complex Tachycardia exists | Confirm that irregular rhythms are explicitly separated from adenosine-eligible regular rhythms. |

## Trauma topics already bundled but worth making explicit

- Avulsed teeth are reasonably placed under Dental Problems; confirm storage/handling instructions.
- Cardiogenic and hypovolemic shock are covered under Hypotension/Shock and cardiac protocols.
- Blunt and penetrating trauma are covered by Multiple Trauma, but visible mechanism-specific branches would improve retrieval.
- Tension pneumothorax appears in shock/traumatic-arrest content; add a direct chest-decompression procedure card.
- Traumatic amputation appears in Extremity Trauma; confirm hemorrhage control, part preservation, and destination instructions are explicit.
- Electrocution/lightning is addressed in Chemical and Electrical Burn; consider retitling or adding search aliases.

## Obstetric coverage

The three Claiborne obstetric protocols bundle most Tennessee topics effectively:

- Childbirth/Labor covers normal delivery and abnormal presentation.
- Newly Born covers neonatal resuscitation, meconium, and APGAR.
- OB-GYN Emergency covers abruption, previa, prolapsed cord, postpartum hemorrhage, and preeclampsia/eclampsia.

**Add / verify:** explicit management of delivery with an intact amniotic sac or amniotic-sac presentation was not found in the structured content.

## Operational and reference items

Existing Claiborne content already addresses refusal/non-transport, field death, termination/withholding, protocol deviation documentation, spinal protection, hospice/palliative care, triage, sepsis, LVAD, life vest, vascular/IO access, tourniquet use, and general radio reporting.

The best app additions are compact reference cards rather than more full protocols:

1. Consent, minors, capacity, and POST.
2. Physician/bystander on scene.
3. Stretcher and patient-movement safety.
4. Mandatory ECG indications.
5. Chest decompression.
6. CPAP.
7. External pacing.
8. Intranasal medication administration.
9. Adult/pediatric/trauma radio templates.
10. GCS, trauma score, and trauma destination.

## Medication reference

Version 2.11.0 includes a searchable medication section with:

- 24 core medication monographs derived from the current Tennessee medication-dosage reference.
- Adult and pediatric dose rows organized by indication.
- EMT, AEMT, and Paramedic filtering.
- High-alert concentration/indication warnings.
- Links back to the applicable Claiborne protocol.
- A visible draft/approval warning.

Before approval, reconcile every medication against the final Claiborne formulary and standing orders. Phase two should add locally adopted drugs not fully represented in the state’s compact dosage table, including ketamine, tranexamic acid, oxytocin, ipratropium, hydroxocobalamin, paralytics, and any approved agitation agents. Optional Tennessee agents such as ketorolac, droperidol, and haloperidol should appear only if Claiborne formally adopts them.

## Recommended build order

1. Safeguarding: family violence/abuse and sexual assault.
2. Chemical/HazMat Exposure with substance cards — completed as TE-09.
3. Eye Trauma and Abdominal/Pelvic Trauma.
4. Spinal Cord Injury / Neurogenic Shock.
5. Physical Restraint and consent/POST operational references.
6. Radio report, trauma destination, chest decompression, and pediatric-reference cards.
7. Cardiac refinements: refractory VF, irregular narrow tachycardia, and PVC decision.
8. Medication formulary reconciliation and medical-director signoff.
